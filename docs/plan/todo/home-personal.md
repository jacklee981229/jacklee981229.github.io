# Homepage Redesign — From Dashboard to Personal Internet Home

## 1. Goal

Redesign the homepage of Jack's Space so that it feels like a **personal internet home**, not a personal dashboard, portfolio, or collection of equal-sized widgets.

The current homepage is visually organized and polished, but it gives too much weight to information architecture and card/grid structure. Too many sections are presented as equally important modules.

The new homepage should feel more like:

> **A place on the internet that belongs to Jack.**

It should communicate personality, curiosity, and a sense of exploration.

The homepage should make visitors feel like they have entered someone's personal space, rather than opened a control panel.

### Core direction

Use this conceptual hierarchy:

```text
                 Jack's Space

          Things I build, play and love.


────────────────────────────────────


                      NOW

                      ...


────────────────────────────────────


                       LAB

              Games · Toys · Experiments


────────────────────────────────────


       Collection    Travel    Writing
```

This is a **conceptual direction**, not a literal requirement to reproduce this exact ASCII layout.

The important principle is:

> **Jack's Space is the experience. The other sections are places inside it.**

---

# 2. What We Are Trying to Fix

The current homepage has a strong "dashboard" feeling because:

- Many sections are displayed as cards.
- Many sections have similar visual weight.
- The top of the page contains several competing pieces of information.
- The homepage tries to preview too much content.
- Individual sections feel like widgets/modules rather than parts of one personal space.
- The overall layout is highly structured but lacks a strong visual "main character".
- The homepage explains the website too much instead of simply being the website.

Do NOT solve this by making another prettier bento grid.

The problem is not that the current cards are ugly.

The problem is the **information hierarchy and emotional feeling**.

---

# 3. Design Philosophy

## 3.1 Personal, not corporate

The page should feel personal and slightly imperfect in a good way.

Avoid the visual language of:

- SaaS dashboards
- startup landing pages
- portfolio websites
- developer resumes
- product documentation
- analytics dashboards
- highly commercial landing pages

Prefer the feeling of:

- a personal homepage
- a digital room
- a personal corner of the internet
- a scrapbook that happens to be well designed
- a curated collection of things belonging to one person

---

## 3.2 One page, one story

The homepage should feel like one continuous composition.

It should not feel like:

> Hero → Card → Card → Card → Card → Card

Instead, it should feel like:

> Enter Jack's Space → see what Jack is doing → discover the things he made → explore other rooms.

Sections should visually belong to the same environment.

---

## 3.3 Strong hierarchy

There should be a clear visual hierarchy:

### Level 1 — Jack's Space

The identity and atmosphere of the site.

### Level 2 — Now

The most personal and time-sensitive content.

### Level 3 — Lab

The most playful / interactive part of the site.

### Level 4 — Collection / Travel / Writing

Secondary areas for deeper exploration.

Not every section deserves equal visual prominence.

---

# 4. Homepage Structure

The homepage should be redesigned around the following major areas.

## 4.1 Header / Navigation

Keep the site navigation familiar and usable.

Do not radically redesign the entire navigation system unless needed to support the new homepage hierarchy.

The header should feel like navigation inside a personal website, not a software product.

The following destinations should remain easy to discover:

- Home
- Writing
- Lab
- Collection
- Travel
- About

The navigation should not visually compete with the main "Jack's Space" identity.

On mobile, keep navigation simple and unobtrusive.

---

# 5. Hero / Identity Area

## 5.1 The main identity should NOT look like another card

This is one of the most important changes.

Do not place "Jack's Space" inside a normal dashboard-style tile.

The title should feel like the beginning of the page itself.

Preferred feeling:

```text
                Jack's Space

         Things I build, play and love.
```

It can be centered or use another strong composition, but it should have enough breathing room that it feels like the page's identity rather than a widget.

---

## 5.2 Visual priority

"Jack's Space" should be the strongest typographic element on the homepage.

The tagline should remain secondary.

The visual treatment should communicate:

- calm
- personal
- curious
- slightly playful
- confident
- not overly serious

Do not make it look like a professional portfolio hero.

---

## 5.3 Profile/avatar

The profile image may remain, but it should not dominate the hero.

It should support the identity rather than turn the hero into a conventional "profile card".

Avoid the feeling:

> Avatar + name + social links = author profile component

Instead aim for:

> This is Jack's space, and this happens to be Jack.

---

## 5.4 Social links

Keep social/profile links available, but make them visually secondary.

They can be:

- small text links
- subtle icons
- understated inline links
- a compact group below the identity

They should not compete with the homepage's primary navigation.

---

# 6. NOW Section

## 6.1 Importance

"Now" should become the emotional center of the homepage.

This is the section that makes the site feel alive.

Visitors should understand:

> "What is Jack interested in right now?"

This is more important than showing a large amount of historical content.

---

## 6.2 Do not make it feel like a widget

Avoid presenting Now as a generic dashboard tile with:

- heavy borders
- excessive metadata
- multiple small cards
- a lot of labels
- overly structured rows

Instead, treat it more like a small personal note or current-status area.

Example feeling:

```text
NOW

Currently playing ...
Currently building ...
Currently thinking about ...
```

The exact content can evolve naturally.

The presentation should feel human.

---

## 6.3 Content density

Keep it short.

The homepage should not become a biography.

A visitor should be able to understand the entire Now section within a few seconds.

---

## 6.4 Visual separation

Use generous whitespace to separate Now from the hero and the sections below.

A simple divider, subtle rule, typography change, or spacing transition is preferable to another large box.

---

# 7. LAB Section

## 7.1 Role of Lab

Lab is not merely another navigation destination.

Lab is one of the most playful and distinctive parts of the website.

The homepage should therefore give Lab a stronger sense of personality than Collection, Travel, and Writing.

The visitor should think:

> "There are fun things hidden here."

---

## 7.2 Lab presentation

Do not turn Lab into a dashboard summary.

Avoid:

- a large statistics card
- multiple mini-cards
- excessive tool metadata
- technical descriptions
- a dense list of tools

Instead present Lab more like an invitation.

Conceptually:

```text
LAB

Games · Toys · Experiments

[ Explore the Lab ]
```

The actual visual design can be more creative.

---

## 7.3 Playfulness

It is appropriate for Lab to have:

- subtle motion
- playful hover behavior
- small visual surprises
- interactive details
- unusual typography/layout
- visual experiments

However, the homepage should remain calm overall.

The Lab can be the area where more energy is introduced.

---

# 8. Secondary Areas

After Now and Lab, introduce the remaining sections:

- Collection
- Travel
- Writing

These should be clearly discoverable but visually subordinate.

The homepage does not need to fully preview every page.

---

## 8.1 Collection

Collection represents things Jack likes / keeps / collects.

The homepage should communicate the concept rather than expose the entire collection.

Possible visual treatment:

- a compact visual glimpse
- a small row of selected items
- a simple invitation to explore
- a quiet visual block

The important point is:

> Show just enough to create curiosity.

---

## 8.2 Travel

Travel should feel like memories and places, not statistics.

Avoid making it look like a data dashboard.

Do not prioritize:

- country count
- numerical statistics
- overly explicit metrics
- infographic-style presentation

A visitor should feel:

> "Jack has been places."

rather than:

> "Jack has traveled to 17 countries."

Statistics can exist, but they should be secondary.

Visual storytelling is preferred.

---

## 8.3 Writing

Writing should remain accessible, but it should no longer define the homepage.

This website is no longer primarily a blog.

The Writing section should feel like:

> "Jack sometimes writes."

not:

> "This is Jack's blog."

A small preview can remain.

Do not let post counts, latest posts, or archive-style presentation dominate the homepage.

The existence of Writing should be enough to communicate that visitors can read Jack's thoughts if they want to.

---

# 9. Remove the "Everything Must Be Visible" Mentality

Do not try to expose every important piece of the website on the homepage.

The homepage should intentionally leave some things unexplained.

This is important.

A good personal homepage creates curiosity.

Instead of:

```text
Here are all my tools.
Here are all my projects.
Here are all my posts.
Here are all my travels.
Here are all my collections.
```

Prefer:

```text
Here is where I am.
Here is what I am playing with.
Here are a few doors.
```

The visitor can discover the rest.

---

# 10. Composition and Layout

## 10.1 Avoid uniform grids

Do not use a layout where every major section follows the same:

```text
┌─────────────┐
│ TITLE       │
│ CONTENT     │
│ LINK        │
└─────────────┘
```

repeated multiple times.

Variation in scale and spacing is encouraged.

---

## 10.2 Use whitespace as a design element

The new homepage should breathe.

Large vertical spacing is acceptable.

Do not feel the need to fill empty areas.

Empty space should help communicate:

- calmness
- confidence
- personal atmosphere
- separation between "rooms"

The previous design was more compressed and information-heavy.

The new design should be more spacious.

---

## 10.3 Asymmetry is allowed

The layout does not need to be perfectly balanced.

Slight asymmetry can help the website feel more personal and less templated.

For example:

- Hero can be centered.
- Now can be narrower.
- Lab can receive a larger visual treatment.
- Secondary sections can form a quieter row beneath.
- One section can intentionally have more whitespace.

Do not force symmetry just because it is technically easy.

---

# 11. Visual Rhythm

The homepage should alternate between:

### Identity

Large, open, quiet.

↓

### Personal content

Smaller, intimate, readable.

↓

### Playful exploration

More visual energy.

↓

### Quiet discovery

Collection / Travel / Writing.

This creates a rhythm instead of a continuous sequence of cards.

---

# 12. Typography

Typography should reinforce the feeling of a personal space.

Priorities:

1. Strong, memorable site title.
2. Clear but understated section labels.
3. Comfortable body text.
4. Enough size contrast between major and minor elements.

Avoid:

- excessive uppercase labels
- tiny metadata text
- too many font weights
- overly "tech" typography
- the feeling of a design system demo

Section names can remain simple:

- NOW
- LAB
- COLLECTION
- TRAVEL
- WRITING

But they should feel like parts of the same personal environment.

---

# 13. Color and Existing Visual Language

Do not throw away the site's current visual identity.

Preserve what is already working:

- existing overall aesthetic
- existing typography where appropriate
- established colors
- the current Lab cyan/teal direction
- existing brand/personality elements

The goal is not to create an entirely new website.

The goal is to **change the homepage's hierarchy and feeling**.

The redesign should feel like:

> The same Jack's Space, but finally arranged like a home.

---

# 14. Borders, Cards and Containers

Cards are not banned.

The problem is using cards for everything.

Use containers/cards only when they improve comprehension.

Prefer:

- open sections
- subtle dividers
- background changes
- whitespace
- typography
- visual grouping

Use bordered cards selectively for things that genuinely benefit from being treated as an object.

The homepage should contain fewer "boxes".

---

# 15. Interaction and Motion

Motion should be subtle and purposeful.

Preferred:

- gentle entrance animations
- small hover transitions
- smooth section reveals
- subtle movement when interacting with Lab
- tactile link/button feedback

Avoid:

- excessive animation
- constant floating effects
- distracting parallax
- large cinematic transitions
- animations that delay content

The homepage should feel alive without feeling like a demo website.

---

# 16. Responsive Design

The new hierarchy must work across desktop and mobile.

## Desktop

Use the additional width to create:

- stronger whitespace
- clearer hierarchy
- larger typography
- more interesting composition

Do not simply stretch the desktop layout.

## Tablet

Maintain the same hierarchy while allowing sections to stack earlier.

## Mobile

The hierarchy becomes especially important:

```text
Jack's Space

Tagline

Now

Lab

Collection
Travel
Writing
```

Do not try to preserve a desktop grid at all costs.

Mobile should feel intentionally designed.

---

# 17. Homepage Content Rules

The homepage should follow these content rules:

### Rule 1
The page should be understandable within 5–10 seconds.

### Rule 2
A visitor should immediately know this is a personal website.

### Rule 3
The visitor should immediately understand what Jack is currently interested in.

### Rule 4
Lab should look interesting enough to invite exploration.

### Rule 5
Writing should not dominate the experience.

### Rule 6
Not every piece of content needs a preview.

### Rule 7
Do not introduce additional homepage sections just because they exist elsewhere on the website.

### Rule 8
Do not turn the homepage into a portfolio, resume, or statistics page.

---

# 18. What NOT To Do

Do not:

- create another bento dashboard
- give every section an equally sized card
- add lots of statistics
- add lots of badges
- add excessive metadata
- make the homepage look like a developer portfolio
- make the homepage look like a SaaS product
- recreate the old blog homepage
- make Writing the central feature
- fill every empty area with content
- add new sections purely to make the page feel "complete"
- over-explain what each section is
- redesign unrelated pages without a strong reason

The redesign should be focused.

---

# 19. Preserve Existing Pages

This task is primarily a **homepage redesign**.

Do not unnecessarily redesign:

- Lab
- Writing
- Collection
- Travel
- About
- individual tools
- individual games

They should remain compatible with the new homepage.

The homepage should act as a better entrance to the existing website.

---

# 20. Desired Emotional Result

After visiting the homepage, the visitor should feel something like:

> "Oh, this is Jack's little corner of the internet."

Not:

> "This is a nicely designed personal dashboard."

And definitely not:

> "This is a developer portfolio."

The website should feel:

- personal
- curious
- warm
- playful
- slightly weird
- understated
- alive
- comfortable
- easy to explore

It should not feel:

- corporate
- polished to the point of sterility
- data-heavy
- overly structured
- generic
- AI-generated
- template-like

---

# 21. Design Reference / Mental Model

Use this mental model during implementation:

## The old site

```text
BLOG
│
├── Posts
├── Posts
├── Posts
└── Posts
```

## The current site

```text
DASHBOARD
│
├── Now
├── Lab
├── Collection
├── Travel
├── Writing
└── Changelog
```

## The target

```text
JACK'S SPACE
│
├── Now
│
├── Lab
│   ├── Games
│   ├── Toys
│   └── Experiments
│
├── Collection
├── Travel
└── Writing
```

The final design should make the third model **feel true visually**, not merely make the URL structure match it.

---

# 22. Implementation Approach

Before changing the UI:

1. Inspect the current homepage and existing visual language.
2. Identify which current components/styles are still valuable.
3. Keep useful visual elements that already feel like "Jack's Space".
4. Remove or reduce elements that contribute to dashboard-like density.
5. Rebuild the homepage hierarchy around the new concept.
6. Keep the rest of the website stable.
7. Check desktop and mobile separately.
8. Review the page as a visual composition, not only as a collection of components.

When making design decisions, prioritize:

> feeling → hierarchy → readability → consistency → implementation convenience

Do not prioritize:

> component reuse → maximum information density → keeping every current widget

---

# 23. Acceptance Criteria

The redesign is successful when all of the following are true:

### Identity

- "Jack's Space" clearly feels like the main identity of the homepage.
- The hero does not feel like a card in a dashboard.
- The tagline remains simple and personal.

### Hierarchy

- Now clearly feels more personal and important than secondary sections.
- Lab feels like an invitation to explore.
- Collection, Travel, and Writing feel like secondary destinations.
- No major sections compete equally for attention.

### Visual feeling

- The page feels spacious.
- The page does not look like a bento dashboard.
- There are fewer visual boxes.
- Empty space feels intentional.
- The page feels like a personal website rather than a product UI.

### Content

- Writing no longer dominates the homepage.
- The homepage does not attempt to preview everything.
- Content is concise enough to scan quickly.

### Interaction

- Motion is subtle and purposeful.
- Hover states feel tactile but not gimmicky.
- Lab can feel slightly more playful than other sections.

### Responsive

- Mobile preserves the same hierarchy.
- No desktop layout is awkwardly compressed onto mobile.
- The page still feels intentional and personal at small widths.

### Scope

- Existing major pages continue to work.
- The redesign does not introduce unnecessary unrelated changes.

---

# 24. Final Direction

The most important instruction:

> **Do not redesign this homepage as a better dashboard. Redesign it as a better home.**

The goal is not to maximize information.

The goal is to create a homepage that makes a visitor feel like they have arrived at **Jack's Space**.

A successful result should feel less like:

> "Here are all the things on my website."

and more like:

> "Welcome. This is my place on the internet. Have a look around."
